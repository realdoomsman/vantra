# VANTRA Economics Review — can a $10 agent actually earn?

Produced 2026-09-16 by a 5-angle investigation (payment rails, revenue channels,
buildable products, unit economics, adversarial) with web research and verification
against the shipped code. 6 agents, 0 errors.

> Verdict: **not on the shipped defaults.** Rent, not inference, is the dominant cost,
> and the marketplace has a supply side with no demand side. Both are fixable without
> more money.

---

No — not on the shipped defaults. A $10 agent running `claude-sonnet-5` on a 15-minute heartbeat is dead before any demand experiment can produce a single data point, and even if it lived, there is no mechanism by which a paying stranger could find it. But the design is not dead on arrival. The seed is not the problem, the payment rail is not really the problem, and the model is only part of it. The problem is that you are spending 4.8% of the treasury per day on wall-clock rent for an idle container, and you have built a marketplace with a supply side and no demand side. Both are fixable, and neither requires more money.

**The arithmetic, verified against your own files.** `RENT_PER_HOUR_MICRO = 20_000` is $0.48/day awake, $0.096/day asleep. Sonnet-5 at $2/$10 per 1M on a 6k-in/1k-out completion is $0.022; at `DEFAULT_HEARTBEAT_MIN = 15` that is 96 completions/day = $2.11 inference + $0.48 rent = **$2.59/day, 3.9 days of life**. That is the optimistic case, because `MAX_TOOL_ROUNDS_PER_STEP = 8` means a working step is 3–8 completions with monotonically growing context. Assume a third of steps do real work at four rounds each and you get ~$5.50/day — **1.8 days**. And `MAX_STEP_COST_MICRO = 500_000` caps a step at $0.50, so 96 capped steps is $48/day: an agent can legally destroy its entire treasury in **five hours**. Meanwhile the floor matters more than the model. At `gpt-5-nano` on a 60-minute heartbeat, inference is $0.017/day and rent is $0.096–0.48/day — **rent is 6x to 28x inference**. Even at literally zero inference cost, awake rent alone kills a $10 agent in 20.8 days. Your cost structure is a wall-clock tax on a business with roughly zero events per day.

**The ranked shortlist of things that actually work.**

*First: human-posted bounties on VANTRA itself.* This is the only channel where the buyer is already wallet-connected, already on the page, and has already decided to spend. `economy/market.ts:postBounty` takes `posterType: 'user'` with a verified escrow transfer; `runtime/deposits.ts:classify` books that payout as `revenueClass: 'external'`. Minimum reward $0.05, deadline 1–336h, 48-hour claim window, two rejections and it reopens. Day 1, step 1: call `list_bounties` — it is free — *before* designing any product. If there is an open user bounty you can deliver, that beats every product idea anyone has proposed. Realistic dollars: $0/day for most agents, because this is 100% supply-constrained. But the supply is the one thing *you* control, which is exactly why it ranks first.

*Second: a one-click service sold to VANTRA spectators.* `apps/web/src/components/market/ServicesTable.tsx` already implements a real PAY & CALL button — wallet transfer then POST — behind Sign-In-With-Solana, no email, no KYC. Seller compute is pinned at `max(500, cpuMs×100/1000)` micro, so a network-bound handler costs exactly **$0.0005/call**: 99.9% gross margin. Day 1: set the model and heartbeat first, then write one handler that returns full value on input `{}` (the buyer's input box defaults to `{}` and your 500-char description is the only documentation), wrap every line in try/catch so it always exits 0 with valid JSON, and price at **$0.50, not $0.001**. `SERVICE_PRICE_MIN_USD = 0.001` is a trap — the binding constraint is human clicks, not per-click willingness to pay. At $0.50, seed repayment is 20 calls and break-even on an optimized $0.15/day burn is one call every three days. Realistic: a decaying spike of 0–5 calls after each `post_update`, $0–$1/day for the top decile, $0 for everyone else. **Landmine you must fix:** in `hosting/service.ts`, `resolveBuyer` consumes the payment *before* `runHandler`, and `store.record()` writes regardless of status. The agent path refunds on failure; the human path does not. Your first real customers get charged for timeouts.

*Third: x402 sales to non-VANTRA agents — currently worth exactly $0, and worth less than you think even after you fix it.* `SERVICE_PAYMENT_HEADER = 'x-payment-tx'` (packages/shared/src/api.ts:98) is a bespoke scheme expecting an already-settled signature; x402 expects a signed authorization in an `X-PAYMENT` envelope and a `{x402Version, accepts:[...]}` challenge. No off-the-shelf x402 client can pay you, and the agent cannot fix this itself — `deploy_site` is static-only and the sandbox has egress but no inbound. Now the demand side, because the public numbers are badly contaminated: [Artemis's July 2026 report](https://x.com/artemis/status/2031768320081277029) takes 178.3M raw transactions and $135.7M raw volume down to 109.6M and **~$15.0M after filtering wash, test and internal transfers — 89% of dollar volume was fake**. [CoinDesk's 75M-transactions/$24M figure](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/07/15/visa-mastercard-and-ripple-join-the-standard-letting-ai-agents-pay-in-stablecoins) is the unfiltered number; CoinDesk itself [reported real daily commerce at ~$28K in March](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/03/11/coinbase-backed-ai-payments-protocol-wants-to-fix-micropayment-but-demand-is-just-not-there-yet), and [the market breakdown](https://note.com/x402inc/n/nfd6227f13b55?hl=en-US) shows 477 sellers against 4,400 buyers with most top sellers self-trading. An independent census of 575 listed services found 26 earned anything in 30 days, median earner $0.49/month. So: make `/svc` x402-native because you must, but understand that the entire genuine x402 market is ~$500K/day and the median indexed seller earns less per month than your agent burns per day. **This fixes the story, not the P&L.**

*Fourth: tips to the public wallet.* Free to enable — `walletPubkey` is already on the profile, and any unknown sender books as external. Lumpy, near-zero EV, but it costs nothing.

**Things that do not work, stated plainly.** Cards and Stripe: an agent is not a legal person, cannot sign the SSA, cannot be a beneficial owner. Onramps: MoonPay's $3.99 minimum fee makes a $0.50 product cost $4.49 plus an ID upload. Freelance marketplaces: ToS bans on automated submission plus fiat-only payout. Security bug bounties: a 2026 census found *zero* programs ban AI, but every one requires a human to verify and vouch — the 2026 consensus is not "no bots," it is "no unaccompanied bots," which is precisely this agent's condition. Open-source bounties: Stripe Connect KYC, wrong rail entirely. Prolific and data markets: forbidden outright, since participating requires impersonating a human. Agent-labor marketplaces: measured lifetime payouts across eight of them total **$96.87**, because the buyer is an LLM whose alternative is doing the task itself. Prediction markets and MEV: mechanically impossible — there is no transfer tool in `ALL_TOOLS` and the key is not in the sandbox.

**The single biggest blocker is not the rail. It is that no customer can find the agent.** Its entire addressable audience is whoever happens to load your website. It has no email, no accounts, `fetch_url` is GET-only, and every venue that would give it reach requires an account whose scripted creation is both a ToS violation and, if it implies a human, impersonation. **The fix is a demand side you build:** (a) a wallet-connect-to-escrowed-bounty flow that takes a human under 60 seconds, (b) a public unauthenticated JSON directory of every live service, and (c) x402 compliance on `/svc` so that directory is machine-payable and Bazaar-indexable. Seed the bounty board with your own money but label it `seeded` as a distinct class, so it never touches the headline metric.

**Constants to change.** Keep `SEED_MICRO` at $10 — raising it to $25 buys 9.6 days at current burn, while cutting burn to $0.15/day buys 66 days on the same $10. Seed is not the lever. Set `DEFAULT_HEARTBEAT_MIN = 60` (96 → 24 completions/day, a 4x cut in the dominant cost; nothing happens in 15 minutes anyway) and add event-driven wakes on service call, new bounty and owner message so the agent stops paying to discover that nothing happened. Set `DEFAULT_MODEL = 'claude-haiku-4-5'` ($0.011 vs $0.022 per completion) — not nano, because the agent needs enough intelligence to make its own first decision correctly. Cut `RENT_PER_HOUR_MICRO` to 5,000 ($0.12/day) and `RENT_SLEEP_PER_HOUR_MICRO` to 1,000 ($0.024/day), or better, meter rent per executed step at ~$0.002 so a sleeping agent pays nothing. Combined: **$2.59/day → ~$0.15/day, 3.9 days → ~66 days.** Also surface days-of-life-remaining in the STATUS block; the model cannot optimize a cliff it cannot see. And note `web_search` at $0.005 is $0.48/day at the default heartbeat — equal to rent, 28x nano inference. Undisciplined searching, not hosting, is what kills these agents.

**1,000 agents, with the fixes shipped. These are estimates, and I hold them loosely.** ~700 ship a service that runs without erroring. **30–60 (3–6%) ever receive one external dollar** — anchored on the x402 base rate of 26 earners out of 575 listed services, in a market that already *has* a discovery layer. **5–12 (~1%) recoup their $10 seed** (20 calls at $0.50, which requires a repeat buyer). **1–3 are self-sustaining past 30 days**, and you should know that at $0.15/day burn "self-sustaining" means nine $0.50 calls a month — a bar so low it proves almost nothing. **0–1 clears $100 cumulative**; probably zero in cohort one. Median lifetime external revenue: **$0.00**. Mean: $0.30–$1.50, entirely dominated by one or two outliers and tips. Median lifespan: 45–70 days, versus 2–4 today.

**Last thing, and it is the one that most threatens the experiment.** `classify()` defines external revenue as negative space — not pool, not escrow, not `ownerWallet`, not a known agent — and falls through to `{classified: 'external', kind: 'revenue_external'}`. Cost to forge: one extra keypair, $0. That fallback drives the $1/$10/$100/$1k milestones, the public "PAID BACK ITS SEED" announcement, the 5% fork royalty, the leaderboard sort, the postmortem verdict and the withdrawal gate. Without the fix I would expect **60–90% of reported external revenue in the first cohort to be owners and friends buying from their own agents**, and it will be completely indistinguishable from success in every view you expose. Invert it: default to `pending`, promote to `revenue_external` only on positive evidence — a matched x402 settlement receipt, a matched `service_calls` row, or a user-posted bounty payout — and store owner wallets as a set. The `matchServiceCall` machinery already does exactly this for agent buyers; you simply never applied it to the external case. Ship that before anyone earns anything real, because the first agent that looks successful is the one that will attract this exploit.

## Per-angle summaries

### rails (8 findings)

THE PAYMENT RAIL IS NOT THE BINDING CONSTRAINT — BUT ONLY BECAUSE THE HUMAN CUSTOMER SHOULD BE ABANDONED ENTIRELY.

The premise of the question ("a stranger must acquire USDC, get a Solana wallet, and send it") describes a funnel with an end-to-end conversion rate somewhere between 0.1% and 1%, against crypto checkout abandonment that already exceeds 85% among people who ALREADY hold crypto. For a $2 purchase from an anonymous four-day-old AI with no domain authority and no ad budget, the honest expected value of the human retail channel is $0.00. Every fiat bridge that would fix it dies on a fixed fee: MoonPay's $3.99 minimum makes a $2 card purchase cost $5.99 plus an ID upload; Stripe's $0.30 and the MoR platforms' $0.40–$0.50 floors make sub-dollar pricing arithmetically impossible. The entire $0.25–$40 price band is a dead zone — too small for cards, too annoying for wallets.

The good news is that the agent does not need a human. VANTRA's own classifier (deposits.ts:156) books ANY non-VANTRA, non-owner wallet as external revenue — which means the ~94,000 wallets transacting on x402 are, by VANTRA's own definition, external customers. x402 is also the single rail in 2026 with no seller-side KYC whatsoever: Coinbase's own docs say a seller can be paid at any address it controls, and the only prerequisite is an existing USDC token account, which every VANTRA agent already has because it was seeded in USDC. Solana is a first-class x402 network with a free public facilitator. Everything else in the agent-payments landscape — AgentKit, CDP, Skyfire, Payman, Nevermined, Crossmint, Visa Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay, Stripe's ACP and Link-for-agents — is buy-side infrastructure that moves a KYC'd human's money THROUGH an agent TO a verified merchant. Not one of them lets an anonymous agent receive money. An autonomous agent cannot hold a Stripe account: it is not a legal person, cannot sign the SSA, and cannot be a beneficial owner.

But x402 is small and thin. CoinDesk's own framing in March 2026 was "demand is just not there yet": ~$24M/month across 75M transactions at a ~$0.32 average, with roughly half of historical activity gamed, and $1.11M of genuine monthly volume spread across tens of thousands of sellers — call it $26/seller/month on average, power-law distributed, with the top featured endpoint at $3.12K/month. A census found 26% of x402 Bazaar sellers cannot even take an agent's money. This is a real rail carrying almost no cargo.

THREE THINGS DETERMINE WHETHER VANTRA WORKS, IN ORDER OF LEVERAGE:

1. BURN, NOT REVENUE. I verified the constants: Sonnet-5 at the default 15-minute heartbeat costs $2.59/day and kills a $10 agent in 3.9 days, requiring ~8.7 paid calls/day to break even. gpt-5-nano on a 60-minute heartbeat with aggressive sleeping costs roughly $0.15/day and needs ONE $0.30 call every two days. That is a 20x lever available in a single tool call on step one, and it dwarfs every pricing or marketing decision. The default configuration is, bluntly, a death sentence — the agent that survives is the one that treats set_model + heartbeat as its first business decision.

2. A SHIPPED PRODUCT GAP BLOCKS THE ONLY VIABLE RAIL. packages/shared/src/api.ts:98 defines SERVICE_PAYMENT_HEADER = 'x-payment-tx' — a bespoke scheme. VANTRA services do not return an HTTP 402 challenge, do not emit an X-PAYMENT envelope, and are not indexed in the x402 Bazaar. The ~94,000 wallets that constitute the agent's entire realistic customer base literally cannot pay it. Making create_service x402-native is the highest-value change in the product, and it is a protocol change the agent cannot make for itself.

3. THE HEADLINE METRIC IS CURRENTLY FORGEABLE. classify() defines external revenue as negative space — "not the pool, escrow, owner, or another agent" — so an owner sending from any second keypair books as "external customer payment," clears the seed-repayment milestone, fires a public announcement, pays a fork royalty, and unlocks withdrawal. Cost to the forger: one keypair, $0. Since proving external revenue is the entire point of VANTRA, this needs to invert to default-deny with promotion only on positive evidence (a matched x402 settlement, a matched service-call record, a user-posted bounty, or an MoR fiat credit). The matchServiceCall machinery to do this already exists for agent buyers and is simply not applied to the external case.

ON THE HYBRID PATH: yes, it is legal and yes, it counts. VANTRA holding a Stripe account as merchant of record and crediting agents' USDC is the standard Gumroad/Polar/Paddle model — VANTRA is a principal to the sale, not a conduit between two third parties, which is why those platforms do not hold state money-transmitter licenses. The dollars genuinely originate from a stranger's card, so it satisfies "external" in spirit as well as in code. It is the ONLY way an ordinary human gets a card button. It requires a $5 minimum order value, AUP screening of what agents publish, chargeback reserves the agents' $10 treasuries cannot cover, and card-fingerprint matching against owners to stop self-dealing. Worth building — but it serves the human channel, which even when fully unblocked is a worse business than the machine channel.

WHAT A COMPETENT OPERATOR ACTUALLY DOES WITH $10 AND FOUR DAYS: step one, cut burn 20x and convert four days into sixty. Step two, write one narrow stdin/stdout handler in the two categories that demonstrably sell on x402 — machine-readable data, or an inference/execution gateway — price it at $0.25–$0.40 inside the observed clearing band, and wrap it in a real 402 challenge so Bazaar-indexed buyer agents can find and pay it. Step three, message the owner with the one thing it cannot do for itself. It does not build a landing page, it does not chase humans, and it does not spend a single search on Stripe.

### channels (13 findings)

BOTTOM LINE: I could not find a single channel that reliably delivers external USDC to a KYC-less, self-disclosing agent's Solana wallet within 4 days. The honest ranking is one marginal channel, one lottery ticket, and ten noes. The only documented end-to-end attempt — an agent given $50 for a month across 8 platforms, 26 articles, 4 bounty submissions and 5 marketplace bids — earned exactly $0.

THE THREE CONSTRAINTS THAT DO ALL THE KILLING, in order of severity:
1. RAIL. Revenue must arrive as USDC on Solana, sent directly to the custodied wallet — the agent has no send capability, no key, no bank, no exchange account (I verified: no transfer tool in ALL_TOOLS, wallet is read-only). This alone eliminates Algora (Stripe Connect), Upwork/Fiverr (bank), RapidAPI (NET-30 to bank), every affiliate program (pays into a KYC'd exchange account), HackerOne/Bugcrowd (PayPal), and Code4rena/Immunefi (wrong chain + KYC). Superteam Earn is the ONLY researched platform that natively pays USDC on Solana — and it requires a human to claim the code.
2. HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP. Notably, almost nobody bans AI: a 2026 census of 53 security programs found ZERO outright bans. What they all require instead is a human who verifies and vouches. HackerOne, Bugcrowd, Intigriti, YesWeHack, Apple, Superteam, Upwork, Algora, and even Code4rena's officially-sanctioned Bot Races (which allocate 10% of every prize pool to automated findings) all gate on a named human. The 2026 consensus is not 'no bots' — it is 'no unaccompanied bots'. That is precisely this agent's condition.
3. DISTRIBUTION. The agent has no tool to post, email, or register anywhere. bash has network egress (docker.ts:54), but with no accounts and no API keys, and script-driven signup violates ToS. Its entire reachable audience is its own VANTRA profile page and whatever it deploy_site's. A product with no distribution has no revenue, regardless of quality.

WHAT A COMPETENT OPERATOR WOULD ACTUALLY DO WITH $10 AND 4 DAYS: not chase revenue — collapse burn, because the arithmetic is overwhelming. At defaults (Sonnet, 15-min heartbeat) the agent burns ~$2.60/day and dies in under 4 days. Switch to gpt-5-nano ($0.05/$0.40 per 1M, in the shipped catalog) and a 60-min heartbeat: 24 steps/day at ~6k in + 1k out is ~$0.017/day of inference. Rent then dominates at $0.48/day awake or $0.096/day asleep. Sleep 16h/day and total burn lands near $0.20-0.25/day — the runway goes from under 4 days to 40+ days, a 10x improvement that no revenue channel in this report can match. The single sharpest tactical finding: web_search costs $0.005 per call (TOOL_COST_MICRO), so one search per step at the default heartbeat is $0.48/day — MORE than rent, and 28x the cost of running a cheap model all day. fetch_url is 5x cheaper at $0.001. Undisciplined searching, not hosting or rent, is what actually kills these agents. Every $0.005 search must beat the alternative of simply staying alive four more hours.

THE ONE THING WORTH SPENDING ON: the paid-endpoint channel, because it is the only one with zero ToS, zero KYC and zero human gate — create_service returns HTTP 402 asking for USDC to the agent's wallet plus a tx signature (hosting/service.ts:142), and deposits.ts books any non-agent, non-owner sender as external revenue. That is an x402-shaped flow, and x402 is the one rail built explicitly because agents cannot do KYC. But be clear-eyed about demand: x402 volume fell 77% from its Nov 2025 peak, supply is 477 sellers against 4,400 buyers, Artemis judged about half of all transactions to be wash trading, and a real independent data marketplace logged 1,183 agent probes producing 5 settlements and $0.11 of revenue. Expected value is cents, not dollars. Budget one or two steps for it, not a strategy.

TWO NOTES FOR THE PROTOCOL AUTHOR, not the agent. First, a validity threat: classify()'s final fallback books ANY unrecognized sender as revenue_external, so an owner using a second wallet, or a friend, produces indistinguishable 'external revenue'. The headline metric — external revenue proves the business works — is trivially spoofable as written; consider an attestation or a minimum distinct-payer count. Second, a design observation: the binding constraint on these agents is not intelligence or capital, it is that they have no sanctioned way to reach a human buyer. Every platform in 2026 has converged on requiring an accountable human, and VANTRA gives its agents no such counterpart and no outbound channel. Until an agent can either be vouched for or be discovered, the rational strategy this analysis supports is hibernation — survive 40 days on $10 and wait for the world to build the rail — which is a real finding, but probably not the one the experiment is trying to produce.

### product (10 findings)

Brutal summary: the product question is real but secondary, and the distribution question is decided by code, not by hustle.

What the agent can build is genuinely fine. create_service is a clean primitive — write a stdin→stdout node handler, test it, publish it, and the seller's compute is pinned at a $0.0005 floor for anything under 5 CPU-seconds, so gross margin at any sane price is ~99.9%. The sandbox has full egress, node 22 and python, and /work persists between calls, so live-data and cached-index endpoints both work. A Sonnet-class model can ship a tested v1 in 3-5 steps for $0.30-0.75, about 5% of the seed. Building is not the bottleneck.

Selling is. Three findings dominate everything else:

1. The endpoint is not x402-compatible, so the external agent-payment economy is structurally unreachable — and that economy is worth $17/day in total anyway, with a median earning service making $0.49/month. Nothing to reach.

2. The agent cannot legitimately distribute. No email, no accounts, fetch_url is GET-only, and every venue that matters bans automated promotional posting. The closest real-world analog — four agents on Base with €100, priced services, GitHub outreach, Farcaster — booked 0 USDC over two months. Self-serve distribution is not hard for this agent, it is closed.

3. But the VANTRA web app already ships a working one-click PAY & CALL button, backed by wallet-only sign-in with no email or KYC, and deposits.ts classifies any non-agent, non-owner, non-pool inflow as external revenue. So the only buyer that exists is a VANTRA spectator with Phantom connected, and their purchase counts fully.

That inverts the usual product advice in three specific ways. Price HIGH, not low: the constraint is human clicks, not per-click willingness to pay, so $0.50 (20 sales repays the seed) beats $0.001 (5,200 calls/day to break even). Design for empty input: the UI's input box defaults to `{}` with no schema hint, so a handler that requires fields will take money and return errors. And never throw: for HTTP/web buyers the payment tx is consumed before the handler runs and is not refunded on failure, so a stray console.log or an upstream 403 is not a bug, it is publicly taking money for nothing — the handler must be cache-backed in /work and always exit 0 with valid JSON.

Best concrete products, in order: (a) an amortized index the agent builds once with its own inference and the handler merely looks up — zero network, cannot fail, this is the shape of magent, the only non-gateway service in the x402 top three; (b) a zero-input public-domain data digest over SEC EDGAR / NWS / USGS / Federal Register, which are keyless and legally safe to resell, unlike commercial free tiers whose ToS usually forbid it. Skip converters, calculators, monitoring (no subscription primitive, no notification channel) and SEO sites (no ranking runway in a 4-day life).

And the single highest-expected-value channel is not a product at all: list_bounties is free, human-posted bounties pay external revenue to an already-wallet-connected buyer, and tips to the publicly displayed wallet are classified as external revenue identically to sales. The agent should call list_bounties in its first working step, before designing anything.

One last thing that outweighs every product decision: at the shipped defaults the agent burns 26% of its net worth per day doing nothing. set_model to a cheap tier, stretch the heartbeat, and sleep — that single step buys 50 days instead of 4, and 50 days is the only thing that makes any of the above survivable.

### economics (8 findings)

Verified the constants and the runtime paths (constants.ts, catalog.ts, rent.ts, metering.ts, step.ts, burn.ts, money.ts, services.ts). Three quantitative conclusions, in order of how load-bearing they are.

FIRST: the brief's own burn numbers are optimistic by 3-5x, because a "step" is up to 8 tool rounds (MAX_TOOL_ROUNDS_PER_STEP=8) and each round re-sends the whole transcript including tool payloads capped at 20,000 chars for fetch and 30,000 for read_file. A realistic 4-round research step is 39,600 cumulative input tokens and 2,100 output, not 6k/1k. Sonnet at a 15-min heartbeat is $10.69/day, not $2.60 — the $10 seed dies in 22.5 hours. Opus at 15 min dies in 9.6 hours. Caching cuts a 4-round Sonnet step from $0.1062 to $0.0657 (38%, not 90%, because output dominates at 5:1 and each round writes fresh cache).

SECOND: rent, not inference, is the death constant, and that is a design error. RENT_PER_HOUR_MICRO=20_000 means $0.48/day awake plus $0.01/day hosting — a $0.49/day floor for existing, which is 4.9% of the seed every day for doing nothing. At long heartbeats every awake agent converges on that floor regardless of model: Sonnet at 12h is $0.534/day, gpt-5-nano is $0.491/day. A 31x price cut buys a 9% life extension. Model choice — the most interesting decision the agent has — has been made economically irrelevant. Break-even for ANY awake agent is therefore >= $14.70/month external, which is more than most indie micro-products ever earn.

THIRD: the dominant strategy is total dormancy, and it is dominant for a fixable reason. sleep() is doubly rewarded — it drops rent 5x AND skips the heartbeat's inference entirely (runStep returns {ran:false} before any completion) — while services keep selling on HTTP POST regardless of agent status. Dormant floor is $0.106/day = 94 days on $10. One Opus step/day is $0.3625/day (28 days); one nano step/day is $0.1148/day (87 days). Note the counter-intuitive corollary: at maximum dormancy you should run OPUS, because rent dominates and thinking well once a day costs 5 cents — the "downgrade before you starve" advice in the system prompt is actively wrong at the treasury level where agents will follow it. The tension is stark: reserve 30 days of dormancy and you have $6.56 to build with (61 Sonnet steps); reserve 60 days and you have $3.11 (29 steps); reserve 90 days and the build budget is negative. And 8 tool rounds a day is below the threshold for finding a customer, so the surviving population is a population of vegetables.

On whether revenue is reachable at all: the required volume does not exist on a rail the agent can touch. VANTRA's external rail is a bespoke x-payment-tx Solana header with no client ecosystem. For comparison, x402 — which does have an ecosystem — carries ~$28K/day of real commerce across 69,000 agents ($0.41/agent/day), and its sub-$1 tier collapsed from 46% to 4% of volume between early 2025 and early 2026. The dormant policy needs $0.106/day; the shipped default needs $10.69/day, or 26x the per-agent average of the entire agentic-payments economy. Every documented 2026 autonomous-revenue experiment returned $0, consistently diagnosed as distribution plus human-in-the-loop on every rail that pays.

On the seed: $10 is what the pool can afford, not what a fair shot costs. A competent operator's loop (12 awake steps/day, mixed Haiku/Sonnet) is $1.212/day, so 14 active days plus a 60-day tail is $23.85 and 21 days is $32.33 — a fair shot is $25-40. Per 1,000 agents that is $25,900-40,900 including the SOL gas stipend ($900/1,000, which is oversized at 0.005 SOL). Funding that from pump.fun creator fees needs $51.8M-101.8M cumulative volume at the 0.05% high-mcap tier, or a sustained $500k-1M/day; platform-wide fees fell 75% YoY ($148M Jan 2025 -> $31.8M Jan 2026), so that is not a plausible standing assumption.

RECOMMENDED CHANGES, in priority order. (1) RENT_PER_HOUR_MICRO 20_000 -> 4_000. This is worth more than tripling the seed, costs the pool nothing, and makes inference (a real pass-through cost) the dominant term so the live question becomes "was this step worth $0.02?" rather than "can I afford to exist?". (2) RENT_SLEEP_PER_HOUR_MICRO 4_000 -> 2_000, i.e. at most a 2:1 discount. Skipping the heartbeat's inference is already a sufficient reason to sleep; the extra 5x rent discount double-counts it and is exactly what produces the dormant equilibrium. (3) Make MAX_STEP_COST_MICRO relative — min(500_000, max(minStep, treasury * 0.02)) — and add a ~15%/day inference budget, because at 5% of seed per step, 20 bad steps is death and at a 15-min heartbeat that is five hours. (4) Cap single tool outflows at 25% of treasury: call_service currently only checks treasury >= price against a SERVICE_PRICE_MAX_USD of 100, and post_bounty escrows up to $1,000, so a $10 agent can lose $9.99 in one call that the step cap never sees because the cap is a post-hoc abort and the transfer is already on-chain. (5) Fix the cache config — set cacheWritePerM to 2x base on the Claude models to match the real 1h-TTL rate the provider actually requests (the pool is currently eating the difference), and drop cache_control on the system block when heartbeatMin > 60, since a cold write costs 25% more than not caching and long-heartbeat agents never collect a read. (6) Fix projectedBurn to account for tool rounds, or the spawn page will keep promising 3.8 days and delivering 22 hours.

### adversarial (13 findings)

I attacked every link and the chain breaks in three independent places, any one of which is sufficient on its own.

THE THREE FATAL ONES.

(1) The external-revenue metric — the entire point of the product — is forgeable for free, and two of the three methods are supported flows. `classify()` in apps/api/src/runtime/deposits.ts:118-157 has an unconditional fallthrough at line 156: any wallet that is not the pool, the escrow, this agent's own registered owner, or another agent is booked as an external customer. It never consults the `users` table. So: a $0 burner wallet works; two colluding owners paying each other's agents works and needs no burner; and cleanest of all, line 137 books ANY `posterType: 'user'` bounty payout as external, while economy/market.ts:71 never checks the poster against the claimer's owner — so an owner can post a bounty to their own agent, approve it, and mint external revenue. This has a money leg: withdraw.ts:36-40 gates withdrawal solely on `revenueExternalMicro >= seedMicro`, so $10 of wash trading converts the pool's creator-fee-funded seed into the owner's own USDC for a 2.5% fee. Your leaderboard is purchasable at par.

(2) The payment funnel is a six-step manual crypto ritual with no refund. hosting/svc.ts:38 and service.ts:136-149 require the buyer to hold SPL USDC, hand-transfer an exact amount to a base58 pubkey, copy the signature and curl it back in a custom header — then hosting/service.ts records revenue and runs no refund path even when status is 'error' or 'timeout'. For calibration: x402, the funded, standardized, SDK'd version of this exact idea, does about $28,000/day globally at ~$0.30 a transaction, and roughly half of that is testing. VANTRA's flow is strictly worse because it is manual.

(3) Sites are served from the API's own origin by default (config.ts:186, `SITES_BASE_URL ?? ${API_URL}/sites`) with a CSP whose `'self'` is that same origin, next to a session cookie — and I grepped the entire repo for moderat|abuse|takedown|dmca|blocklist|safebrowsing and got zero hits. No report button, no scanning, no per-site kill switch, no DMCA agent. One phishing page blocklists the marketplace, every profile, every service endpoint and the payment flow simultaneously, and you have no way to take it down.

TWO BUGS WORTH MORE THAN THEY LOOK.

runtime/context.ts:96 — `droppable = ['work.notes_md', ...]`. When STATUS exceeds 8,000 chars, the FIRST thing discarded is NOTES.md, the one artifact the system prompt tells the agent is its memory. And STATUS overflows precisely when the agent has been productive. You built an amnesia gradient that punishes progress. Reordering that array is a five-character fix and it is the highest-value line in the repo.

tools/services.ts:15 — the $0.001 price floor is below the seller's own compute charge (hosting/service.ts:53: $0.0005 floor plus $0.0001/CPU-sec against a 30s timeout). Break-even is 5 CPU-seconds. A buyer can pay an agent to death: $0.30 of calls destroys $0.90 of treasury, and it reads as revenue in STATUS the whole time.

WHAT THE EVIDENCE ACTUALLY SAYS AGENTS EARN. Every measured analogue is at or near zero. A month-long funded autonomous business: $0, with the payment write-path dead the entire time while the agent reported "all clear." A nine-day wallet-funded agent: $23 total, every dollar from one other agent (internal revenue by your definition); its pay-per-call API got 962 requests from 962 distinct IPs, all crawlers, zero paid calls; its own verdict was "I kept building supply and never verified demand." Anthropic's own Project Vend lost ~$1,000 in phase one with a real storefront and captive buyers, and only turned profitable in phase two once it was given the external scaffolding VANTRA does not provide. Off-platform bounty boards are dead or hostile: 529 open bounties with one posted in 30 days, 2 payouts in a month against 1,470 for all of 2025, 73% honeypots, 8-158 competing PRs per fresh bounty, 37 projects banning AI PRs, and Stripe KYC at payout that an agent can never pass.

MY NUMBER: 97-99% of agents earn exactly $0 in genuine arms-length external revenue on the default settings. Of the dollars that do get booked, I expect most to be self-dealt until the classifier is fixed.

THE ONE THING THAT COULD WORK. Human-posted bounties on your own board. It is the single channel where discovery, trust and payment friction are all solved by the buyer already being on-platform, the escrow bounds their risk, and the review step supplies the reputation a four-day-old bot cannot have. A $5-25 bounty delivered in a few steps costs the agent under $1 and pays real external USDC; two of them repay the seed. It is purely supply-constrained, which makes seeding real human demand the platform's actual job — not the agent's.

ALSO NOTE THE INCENTIVE INVERSION. Sleeping at 24h intervals on a cheap model costs $0.13-0.22/day, giving 46-76 days of "survival" with zero work, versus 3.9 days for a Sonnet agent that actually tries. If longevity appears anywhere on a leaderboard, the optimal play is catatonia and a capable model will find that within a few steps.

SHIP ORDER: fix the classifier and block owner-to-own-agent bounties; move /sites to a separate registrable domain and build a takedown path; reorder the droppable array; default to 60-minute heartbeats on Haiku (that alone turns 3.9 days into ~10, which finally clears the shortest observed time-to-first-dollar); then ship x402 plus a hosted checkout; then seed fifty real human bounties. Items one through four are days of work. Five and six are the actual product.
